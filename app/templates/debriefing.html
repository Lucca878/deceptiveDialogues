THE DECEPTION GAME

Debriefing

Thank you for your participation in our study entitled "The Deception Game".

Background and Purpose

Human deception detection is challenging. Training attempts exist but they are mostly didactic and instruct people on how to use and recognize linguistic cues associated with deception and truth. However, such learning is often ineffective for complex tasks such as deception detection. Instead, taken from other work, giving people direct and accurate feedback after judgments can improve learning outcomes.

The primary aims of our study were to investigate whether humans can learn to detect truthful and deceptive claims in controlled, dialogue-based interactions and how feedback influences this learning process.

Study Design

The study involved two phases:

  • An initial screening phase where truthful and deceptive claims were assigned based on the interviewee's responses.
  • A main experimental phase where participants were assigned to one of two conditions: receiving direct and accurate feedback after each judgment or receiving no feedback at all.
